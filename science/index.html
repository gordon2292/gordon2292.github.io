01

Homeostasis and feedback loops

The central idea for this unit

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a stable internal environment within safe limits. Conditions still change slightly around a normal range, so homeostasis is dynamic rather than perfectly fixed.

StimulusSensorControl centreEffectorResponse

Negative feedback

The response counteracts the original change. It moves the condition back towards its normal range.

Examples: body temperature and blood glucose.

Positive feedback

The response increases the original change. The cycle continues until a clear endpoint stops it.

Examples: blood clotting and childbirth.

Common correction

In negative feedback, the initial stimulus is reduced or stopped. It is not amplified.

02

Endotherms, ectotherms and thermoregulation

How organisms control temperature

Endotherm

An animal that produces most of its body heat through metabolism. Mammals and birds can keep a fairly constant internal temperature, but this requires more food energy.

Ectotherm

An animal whose body temperature depends more on external heat. Reptiles and amphibians often move between warmer and cooler places to control temperature.

When an endotherm becomes too hot

  1. Stimulus: body temperature rises above the normal range.
  2. Sensor: thermoreceptors in the skin and hypothalamus detect the rise.
  3. Control centre: the hypothalamus coordinates a response.
  4. Effectors: sweat glands release sweat and skin arterioles widen.
  5. Response: evaporation and increased heat loss lower body temperature.

When an endotherm becomes too cold

The hypothalamus activates skeletal muscles and skin arterioles. Shivering produces heat, while vasoconstriction reduces heat loss from the skin.

Use the right term

Vasodilation means wider skin arterioles. Vasoconstriction means narrower skin arterioles.

03

The endocrine system

Hormones and target cells

The endocrine system is made of glands that secrete hormones into the bloodstream. Hormones travel through the body, but only target cells with the matching receptor respond.

SignalHormone
RouteBloodstream
SpeedUsually slower than nerves
EffectOften lasts longer

Glands to recognise

Pituitary gland
Releases hormones that control other endocrine glands. The hypothalamus helps regulate pituitary activity.
Thyroid gland
Produces hormones that affect metabolic rate.
Adrenal glands
Release hormones involved in the stress response.
Pancreas
Uses insulin and glucagon to regulate blood glucose.

Nervous control

Electrical impulses move along neurons. Responses are usually rapid and short.

Endocrine control

Hormones move in blood. Responses are usually slower and can last longer.

04

Blood glucose regulation

Insulin, glucagon and the pancreas

Blood glucose regulation is a negative feedback process. The pancreas acts as a sensor and control centre.

After a carbohydrate-rich meal

High blood glucose is detected by beta cells in the pancreas. They secrete insulin. Body cells take up more glucose, and the liver converts glucose to glycogen. Blood glucose falls towards its normal range.

Between meals or during exercise

Low blood glucose is detected by alpha cells in the pancreas. They secrete glucagon. The liver breaks down glycogen and releases glucose. Blood glucose rises towards its normal range.

GlucoseA simple sugar used in respiration
GlycogenThe storage form of glucose
HyperglycaemiaBlood glucose is too high
HypoglycaemiaBlood glucose is too low

Type 1 diabetes

The pancreas makes little or no insulin because insulin-producing cells have been destroyed.

Type 2 diabetes

Target cells respond less effectively to insulin, and insulin production may also decrease.

05

Nervous system and reflexes

Fast communication through neurons

The nervous system detects internal or external changes. The central nervous system processes the information before coordinating a response.

Central nervous system

The brain and spinal cord. It receives information and coordinates responses.

Peripheral nervous system

The nerves outside the brain and spinal cord. It carries signals to and from the CNS.

Neuron roles

Sensory neuron
Carries an impulse from a receptor towards the CNS. It is also called an afferent neuron.
Relay neuron
Connects neurons inside the CNS. It is also called an interneuron.
Motor neuron
Carries an impulse away from the CNS to an effector. It is also called an efferent neuron.
Effector
A muscle or gland that produces the response.

Withdrawal reflex

Pain receptorSensory neuronRelay neuronMotor neuronMuscleHand withdraws

The spinal cord coordinates the immediate withdrawal. Information also reaches the brain, allowing the person to become aware of pain.

Across a synapse

  1. An electrical impulse reaches the presynaptic axon terminal.
  2. Vesicles release a neurotransmitter into the synaptic gap.
  3. The neurotransmitter diffuses and binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane.
  4. A new electrical impulse may begin in the next neuron.
Direction memory aid

Afferent approaches the CNS. Efferent exits the CNS.

06

Salinity and osmoregulation

Water movement across cell membranes

Osmoregulation controls water and solute concentrations inside an organism. Most seawater has a salinity close to 35 parts per thousand, although salinity changes near river mouths and where evaporation is high.

Diffusion

The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

Osmosis

The net movement of water across a partially permeable membrane from higher water concentration to lower water concentration.

Isotonic solution
The solution has the same effective solute concentration as the cell, so there is no net water movement.
Hypotonic solution
The solution has a lower solute concentration than the cell. Water moves into the cell.
Hypertonic solution
The solution has a higher solute concentration than the cell. Water moves out of the cell.
Osmotic pressure
The pressure needed to prevent the net movement of water by osmosis.

Marine bony fish

They tend to lose water and gain salts. They drink seawater and remove excess salts through the gills and kidneys.

Freshwater fish

They tend to gain water and lose salts. They release large amounts of dilute urine and actively take up ions through the gills.

07

Plant responses

Tropisms, seasonal change and defence

A tropism is directional growth in response to a stimulus. Positive tropism is growth towards the stimulus, while negative tropism is growth away from it.

Phototropism
Growth in response to light. Auxin can cause greater cell elongation on the shaded side of a shoot, bending it towards light.
Gravitropism
Growth in response to gravity. Roots normally show positive gravitropism, while shoots show negative gravitropism.
Hydrotropism
Growth in response to water availability. Roots often grow towards moister soil.
Thigmotropism
Growth in response to touch, such as a climbing plant wrapping around a support.
Chemotropism
Growth in response to a chemical concentration.

Seasonal responses

Photoperiod is the duration of daylight. Plants use light information and hormones to coordinate flowering, leaf fall and other seasonal events. The timing of recurring biological events is called phenology.

Physical defence

Thorns, bark, waxy surfaces and hairs can reduce feeding or limit tissue damage.

Chemical defence

Plants can make substances that taste unpleasant, irritate skin or interfere with an herbivore's development.

08

Immune defence and treatment

Barriers, immune cells and medicines

Lines of defence

  1. First line: skin, mucus and other barriers reduce pathogen entry.
  2. Second line: inflammation and phagocytes provide a rapid non-specific response.
  3. Third line: lymphocytes recognise specific antigens. B cells produce antibodies, while memory cells support a faster later response.

Antigens are molecules recognised as foreign by the immune system. An antibody has a binding site with a shape that matches a particular antigen.

Antibiotics

Treat bacterial infections. They do not kill viruses. Misuse can increase selection for resistant bacteria.

Antivirals

Reduce viral replication or interfere with a viral process. They usually target particular viruses.

Vaccines

Expose the immune system to antigens safely, leading to memory cells and a faster response after later exposure.

Herd protection

High vaccination levels can reduce transmission, helping protect people who cannot receive a particular vaccine.

Do not confuse these terms

Antigens are recognised as foreign. Antibodies are proteins made by B cells that bind to specific antigens.

Graphs and CER

How to earn marks from evidence
C

Claim

Give a direct answer to the question.

E

Evidence

Quote a figure or pattern with units.

R

Reasoning

Explain how the science connects the evidence to the claim.

Graph routine

  1. Read both axes and the key before interpreting the graph.
  2. State the pattern and name both variables.
  3. Quote data from the start and end of the pattern.
  4. Include the unit shown on the axis.
  5. Explain the result using feedback-loop science.

Claim: Blood glucose regulation became less effective at higher injected glucose concentrations.

Evidence: Blood glucose remained near 10 mmol dm-3 up to about 85 mmol dm-3 injected, then increased above this level.

Reasoning: The rising blood glucose suggests that the normal negative feedback response could no longer return the concentration to its earlier level.

Inquiring and designing

Planning a scientific investigation

Maximum: 8

Students explain a testable problem, formulate a hypothesis using scientific reasoning, control variables, plan relevant data collection and design a safe investigation.

For 7-8: the problem and variable control are explained, the hypothesis uses correct scientific reasoning, and the method is logical, complete and safe.

Processing and evaluating

Using data and judging an investigation

Maximum: 8

Students present and transform data, interpret results using scientific reasoning, evaluate the hypothesis and method, then explain useful improvements or extensions.

For 7-8: data are correctly organised, transformed and presented. The evaluation uses the investigation's outcome and the proposed improvements directly address identified limits.

Science in society and source use

Maximum: 8

For 7-8: the response explains the application, discusses and evaluates implications through a relevant factor, communicates precisely and documents sources completely.
